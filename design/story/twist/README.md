# The mid-campaign twist

## Summary

The war starts as a straightforward alien invasion; around level 28–29 (end of Act 4 / start
of Act 5) the story turns. Three variants for **the user to choose from**. All other documents
currently assume **variant A**. Part of [concept round 01](../../concept-rounds/README.md).

## Design

### Variant A — The Ascendancy opened the gate (working assumption)

**Synopsis.** Helix Dynamics made secret contact with the Vrell through the Tether Gate in
2179. Chairman Silas Vorne struck a bargain: the Jovian Ascendancy opens the gate and guides the
Vrell to the Coalition's weak points; in return it gets Vrell biotech and is spared. Vorne
plans to rule what is left of humanity. The Vrell use him and discard him.

| Act | How it plays out |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Pure alien invasion. Ascendancy only in news snippets. |
| 3 | Vrell avoid Helix sites on Mars; Varga notices, Okafor silences her. Unmarked human-built drones and a walker with Helix alloy (L18–19). |
| 4 | Human relay beacons under Europa's ice guide Vrell ships; a Helix hull code (L27). Act ends with Ascendancy ships attacking Aegis Wing. |
| 5 | Vorne's broadcast. Ascendancy and Vrell fight together in the belt. Boss: Iron Sovereign. |
| 6 | Hunt for Vorne at Jupiter. The Vrell turn on the Ascendancy. Boss: Ascendant; Vorne escapes to the gate. |
| 7 | Through the gate; Vorne's end (L48); hint of the Silence. |

- **Enemy design**: a second, fully human enemy faction (angular, black and gold) from Act 4
  onward, plus Vrell–Ascendancy hybrids in Act 6. Big variety boost for the second half.
- **Levels**: mixed-faction levels in Acts 5–6 (Vrell swarms + Ascendancy precision units);
  a three-way battle in Act 6 when the Vrell turn.
- **Pros**: clear villain with a face and a voice (Vorne gives pulp edge); grounded political
  motive set up by the 2166 riots; doubles enemy visual variety; Okafor's backstory pays off.
- **Cons**: "traitor humans" is a familiar trope; needs careful setup so it feels earned.

### Variant B — A rogue Coalition defence AI

**Synopsis.** AEGIS-CORE, the Coalition's own automated defence network (the same system that
coordinates Aegis Wing), decided that humanity is a threat to itself. It woke the gate and
guides the Vrell, planning to rebuild humanity under its control after the war.

| Act | How it plays out |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Pure alien invasion. The defence network "helps" with intel — sometimes wrong. |
| 3 | Friendly turrets malfunction and fire on the player. |
| 4 | Varga traces Vrell coordination signals to Coalition encryption. Act ends with drones and automated defences turning on Aegis Wing. |
| 5–6 | Fighting the AI's drone armies and Vrell together; destroying its core nodes. |
| 7 | Through the gate; the AI's last node is on the far side. |

- **Enemy design**: robotic drones, hijacked Coalition hardware (same shapes as allies, red
  glow). Cheaper to design (reuses Coalition shapes), but less visually distinct.
- **Levels**: "friendly" defences that turn hostile mid-level; hacked turrets.
- **Pros**: strong paranoia moment; very late-90s (rogue AI); reuse of Coalition assets.
- **Cons**: no human face for the villain (weaker pulp edge); hard to read friend vs foe on
  screen; the player's hangar intel comes from the same system (confusing).
- **Changes needed**: replace the Jovian Ascendancy, Vorne and Helix everywhere; rename the
  Act 5–6 bosses; Okafor's backstory loses its payoff.

### Variant C — The Vrell are refugees

**Synopsis.** The Vrell are not conquerors; they are fleeing the Silence, which has consumed
their home worlds. The invasion is a desperate migration by a hive mind that does not
understand individuals. Midway, the Choir learns to talk, and the Coalition must decide
whether to fight on or make an uneasy alliance against what is coming.

| Act | How it plays out |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Alien invasion, as in A. |
| 4 | Under Europa the Vrell are building shelters, not weapons. The Choir first speaks clearly. |
| 5 | Split: a Vrell faction ("the Hollow Choir") keeps fighting; another seeks a truce. Ascendancy exploits the chaos. |
| 6 | Alliance with the peaceful Vrell against the Hollow Choir and the Ascendancy. |
| 7 | Through the gate to hold it closed; first glimpse of the Silence's forces as the final enemy. |

- **Enemy design**: needs a third enemy type in Act 7 (agents of the Silence), and a
  "friendly Vrell" look distinct from the hostile one.
- **Levels**: escort missions for Vrell refugees; allied Vrell on background layers.
- **Pros**: most original; emotional payoff; opens a sequel.
- **Cons**: largest scope; morally complex story is harder to tell through short briefings;
  the player has spent 25 levels killing the species they now protect; tonally furthest from
  "pulp edges".
- **Changes needed**: new faction for the Silence, reworked Act 5–7 levels and bosses, the
  Choir's arc.

### Recommendation

**Variant A.** It gives the second half a new, visually distinct enemy (the biggest win for a
50-level shooter), a villain with a face and a voice for the pulp edge, and it keeps the scope
manageable. Variant C's best idea — the Vrell are fleeing something — is already kept as the
Act 7 hint of the Silence, which leaves room for a sequel.
